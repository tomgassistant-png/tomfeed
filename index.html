▶️ Seniors Get Rich, The Young Go Broke

2026-11-13T12:03:00Z · YouTube · Peter St. Onge · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AAHCmfOZ9ho

A Federal Reserve study, as Peter St. Onge presents it, finds households over 75 got almost 40 percent wealthier in the last three years, to a record median of about half a million dollars, while households under 35 lost a quarter of their wealth, down to about $33,000, after inflation. He argues the Fed's near-zero rates and heavy asset buying during Covid sent houses and stocks up for people who already owned them, then higher rates left existing owners with cheap mortgages and first-time buyers with much larger payments. Nearly 20 percent of families reported a late loan payment in 2025, up from 12 percent in 2022, and the share spending at least 40 percent of income on debt rose to 8.6 percent. He says this is not seniors' fault: they saved and invested, while Social Security, on his account, returned far less than a privately owned IRA would have. His remedy is to end the cycle of deficits, asset inflation, and emergency rate hikes rather than tax older households to close the gap.
