Off Leash update 28 October 2026 ISRAEL •With massive turnout and about 75% of votes counted, Israel’s election appears to be deadlocked, with both Prime Minister Netanyahu and his main rival, Gadi Eisenkot, claiming a lead. •Netanyahu’s Likud party is on track to win more Knesset seats than any other party (28), but its loss of eight seats compared to the 2020 election represents a minor rebuke to Netanyahu and his policies. •Meanwhile, Likud’s coalition is projected to win 56 seats to Eisenkot’s bloc’s 48, putting both short of the 61 needed to form a government. If that happens, Pres. Herzog anoints either Netanyahu or Eisenkot to try to form a government by recruiting enough new coalition partners to clear the 61-seat hurdle. Analysts predict both would come up short, in which case Netanyahu would stay on as a caretaker until new elections pick a clear leader. •A clearer outcome from yesterday’s election was the surge in support for Israel’s two major Arab parties, which won a combined 16 seats – six more than the 10 they hold now, and the entire balance of seats that Netanyahu and Eisenkot would be fighting over to reach a majority. Thus, neither Netanyahu nor Eisenkot will be able to form a government without winning over at least one of the Arab parties. Netanyahu has vowed not to partner with Arab parties, and Eisenkot has said he’d only consider doing so if they agree to thorny concessions like accepting that Hamas is a terrorist organization. BRAZIL •Flávio Bolsonaro and his family fled Brazil after the Supreme Court ordered him to surrender his passports and then ordered them cancelled as part of a money-laundering investigation. UKRAINE •Jonas Gahr Store and Alexander Stubb – the prime minister of Norway and president of Finland, respectively – held a 90-minute call with Russia’s Pres. Denmark’s Prime Minister, Mette Frederiksen, said she was “very skeptical” of the dialogue and couldn’t see “any signs that Russia wants peace,” while Sweden’s Prime Minister, Ulf Kristersson, chided: “Holding talks that risk dividing a united European voice is risky.” •European leaders generally agree that it would be better to speak as one voice – and Pres. Putin has even encouraged them to align internally before coming to him – but they’ve struggled to align on what their unified position would be and who should speak for it. •In any case, Store said the call was “familiar” and bore no breakthroughs: Putin “goes through how he sees this conflict as having developed since 2014.
