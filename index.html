Off Leash update 10 November 2026 CHINA •Presidents Trump and Putin are due to meet Xi Jinping next week, and the staffers organizing the heavyweight trilateral can’t even agree on how their principals are going to disagree when they meet. •China is reportedly pushing for it to be a highly scripted affair, in which each leader would read a pre-circulated text and the other two would nod approvingly. The U.S. side wants Trump to be able to wing it and raise topics that weren’t on the pre-agreed agenda – including, some think, nuclear arms control. That’s a non-starter for Xi, who doesn’t want China to be restricted to its current, inferior arsenal. •Separately, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang’s admission to an extramarital affair with Alice Huang – an executive alleged to be a Chinese spy – is reviving scrutiny over Huang’s company, Megaspeed, and other Asia-based firms that U.S. officials suspect are defying strict rules to smuggle advanced chips into China. •Megaspeed indeed seems a bit dodgy: when U.S. officials and reporters visited its locations, they noted that none of the chips the company had imported were actually in use, and the vast majority remained in re-shippable packaging. When they asked an employee what her company actually does, the employee responded: “It’s not clear.” Another thing that’s unclear is why Megaspeed was allowed to continue buying advanced chips after that investigation, which took place over a year ago. IRAN •It seems Pres. Trump is heeded the damning verdict voters passed on his Iran war via a midterm drubbing of his party’s candidates. He told CNN that it’s “highly unlikely” he’ll resume strikes on Iran – as long as negotiations on Iran’s nuclear program and reopening the Strait of Hormuz continue to make good progress. That said, those negotiations haven’t made much progress at all, and he’s likely to run out of patience. •Meanwhile, France and the U.S. both blamed Iran for recent cyberattacks on French grocery refrigerators. The French Foreign Ministry summoned Iran’s Ambassador to France to “strongly protest against this extremely serious act.” Iran hasn’t officially responded yet but will probably deny France’s claim. BANGLADESH •Bangladesh’s ousted leader, Sheikh Hasina, applied for a passport and identity card to return to her home country from self-imposed exile in India. •The new government – which has convicted Hasina on charges of mass murder and terrorism, sentenced her to death in absentia, and banned her Awami League party – rejected her application. It wants India to extradite her instead of facilitating a return she would surely frame as triumphant. •Hasina insists she’ll return in December – with or without the proper papers – because she would rather face justice than remain exiled from her home country. Her critics think she’s returning to galvanize her leaderless party’s supporters and challenge the government that replaced her. UKRAINE •Ukrainian and Belarusian hackers said they’d broken into Russia’s statistics agency, Rosstat, and found death certificate data showing that 400,000 Russians have died in the Ukraine war. Analysts think that could still be an underestimate.
