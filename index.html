Trump has noted, some oil is still getting out of the Gulf, but that’s largely because of an unsustainable U.S. military operation to escort ships. It’s also extremely pricy oil, because risk has pushed maritime insurance premiums skywards. UKRAINE •Ukraine’s state electricity company announced “new power cuts” resulting from Russian attacks on power plants this week. •U.S. officials anonymously told the NYT that the energy ceasefire Pres. Trump announced on Sunday covers oil refineries but not power plants, which could then be included in a broader follow-on truce – assuming this one goes well. •If true, Russia’s strikes on Ukrainian power plants technically don’t violate Trump’s energy ceasefire (which neither Russia nor Ukraine have confirmed they agreed to anyway). But it would make it a bad deal for Ukraine, which has no fully functional oil refineries (and only one that partly functions) but relies on its power plants to heat homes and run businesses. •Pres. Zelensky said Ukraine received no warnings this week about incoming ballistic missiles from the Western intelligence partners who usually sound alarms in advance. Other Ukrainian officials later clarified that the lack of early warning wasn’t a case of neglectful allies but rather due to technical issues and / or Russia’s use of hard-to-spot missiles. FALKLANDS •British Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband signed off on a big new oil drilling project near the Falkland Islands, fueling a long-simmering dispute with Argentina over the archipelago, which Argentina also claims (and calls the Malvinas). •Argentina quickly asked an international maritime tribunal to intervene to “preserve Argentina’s rights.” •That’s unlikely to stop the project from moving forward towards production in 2028: the tribunal’s rulings are legally binding, but it has no way to enforce them if a country declines to comply – as the UK surely will.
